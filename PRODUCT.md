# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Developers evaluating whether to install ReviewDesk for their own GitHub and Jira review workflow — visitors arriving from GitHub, X, Hacker News, or a teammate's word of mouth. Technical, skeptical of marketing fluff, and looking to see the actual product fast.

## Product Purpose

The landing page exists to get a developer from "what is this" to "downloading it" in one scroll, by showing the real app UI — not illustrations — and being specific about the problem it solves: a review queue scattered across GitHub, Jira, and browser tabs. Success is App Store adoption downstream of this page: downloads and organic discovery driven by a page that reads as credible to an engineering audience.

## Positioning

The reviews that need you, always in sight — a native Mac app, not another web dashboard, built by an engineer who was tired of tab-hunting for pull requests.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: "Download for macOS." Secondary CTA: "See how it works," which scrolls to the feature and process sections.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: pull requests that need you, always visible on the Mac, no browser tabs required.
- Belief ladder: this understands the exact pain of hunting PRs across tabs, then it actually connects to my real tools (GitHub, Jira) automatically, then it's trustworthy with my credentials (Keychain, no backend, no telemetry), then it's a real, native, well-built Mac app rather than a wrapper, then downloading costs nothing so it's worth trying.
- Proof on hand: none yet — the page is pre-launch. Real product screenshots (board, detail, repositories views) currently stand in as proof. Revisit this section once reviews, ratings, or install numbers exist.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, engineering-grade. Dark theme, a single accent (merge green), monospace micro-labels, restrained motion — a page that reads like a well-built developer tool, not a startup pitching itself.

## Anti-references

Not SaaS-cream. Not the gradient-hero-metric template. No tiny uppercase eyebrows stacked above every section. No bouncy or elastic motion. No glassmorphism used as decoration. The page should never look like it was assembled from a landing-page kit.

## Design Principles

Show, don't tell: real product screenshots over illustrations or abstract mockups. One accent, used consistently: the merge-green signal means the same thing — readiness, success — everywhere it appears, in the app and on the page. Technical credibility over sales language: copy stays specific (ticket keys, badge conditions, refresh intervals) rather than adjective-heavy. Restraint as a brand signal: one dark theme, one accent, quiet motion — the opposite of a loud SaaS pitch.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast on the dark theme, a full reduced-motion fallback for scroll reveals and the marquee/pulse animations (already present in `globals.css`), and a keyboard-navigable nav and FAQ accordion.
